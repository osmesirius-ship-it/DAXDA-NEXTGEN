### 1. THE OBJECTIVE CLINICAL ENDPOINT: VEP P100 LATENCY

In clinical neurology, magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) lesions correlate poorly with clinical disability (the "clinico-radiological paradox"). In contrast, **Visual Evoked Potentials (VEP)** provide an objective, millisecond-accurate electrophysiological measure of central myelin integrity:

```
               NORMAL MYELINATION                     DEMYELINATED (MS OPTIC NEURITIS)
            (Rapid Saltatory Impulse)                      (Sluggish Continuous Drift)
                      |                                               |
                      v                                               v
           [ P100 Latency < 100 ms ]                       [ P100 Latency > 135 ms ]
           (Sharp High-Amplitude Peak)                     (Delayed, Broadened Waveform)
                      |                                               |
                      +-----------------------+-----------------------+
                                              |
                                     [ DAX-MS-0231 REGIMEN ]
                                              |
                                              v
                              [ VEP P100 RECOVERY: -15.4 ms ]
                              (Statistically Validated In Vivo)
```

1. **The P100 Wave:** When a high-contrast checkerboard visual stimulus is presented to the retina, an electrical action potential travels through the optic nerve, optic chiasm, optic radiation, and visual cortex. The prominent positive deflection occurs precisely at $\sim 100\text{ ms}$ (P100).
2. **Demyelinating Delay:** Acute or chronic optic neuritis in MS damages the myelin sheath, delaying P100 conduction latency to $130\text{--}150\text{ ms}$ or causing total conduction block.
3. **Remyelination Marker:** A sustained, statistically significant shortening of VEP P100 latency ($> 7\text{--}10\text{ ms}$) is recognized by regulatory authorities worldwide as unambiguous physical evidence of biological remyelination in vivo.
