## 2. Differential Geometry on $\mathcal{M}^4$

### 2.1 Riemannian Metric and Levi-Civita Connection
Let $\mathcal{M}^4$ be equipped with a symmetric positive-definite $(0,2)$-tensor field $g = g_{\mu\nu} dx^\mu \otimes dx^\nu$.

The metric determinant and inverse metric satisfy:
$$g = \det(g_{\mu\nu}), \quad g^{\mu\sigma} g_{\sigma\nu} = \delta^\mu_\nu$$

The unique torsion-free, metric-compatible Levi-Civita connection coefficients (Christoffel symbols of the second kind) are:
$$\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu} = \frac{1}{2} g^{\lambda\sigma} \left( \frac{\partial g_{\nu\sigma}}{\partial x^\mu} + \frac{\partial g_{\mu\sigma}}{\partial x^\nu} - \frac{\partial g_{\mu\nu}}{\partial x^\sigma} \right)$$

### 2.2 Geodesic Stepping
A geodesic curve $\gamma(t): [0, 1] \to \mathcal{M}^4$ satisfies the Euler-Lagrange extremum of path length:
$$\frac{d^2 x^\lambda}{dt^2} + \Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu}(x) \frac{dx^\mu}{dt} \frac{dx^\nu}{dt} = 0$$

Riemannian path length is computed as:
$$L(\gamma) = \int_0^1 \sqrt{g_{\mu\nu}(x) \frac{dx^\mu}{dt} \frac{dx^\nu}{dt}} \, dt$$

### 2.3 Curvature Tensors
The Riemann curvature $(1,3)$-tensor measures non-commutativity of covariant derivatives $[\nabla_\mu, \nabla_\nu] v^\rho = R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} v^\sigma$:
$$R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu \Gamma^\rho_{\nu\sigma} - \partial_\nu \Gamma^\rho_{\mu\sigma} + \Gamma^\rho_{\mu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda_{\nu\sigma} - \Gamma^\rho_{\nu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\sigma}$$

Contracting indices yields the Ricci $(0,2)$-tensor and Ricci scalar:
$$R_{\mu\nu} = R^\lambda{}_{\mu\lambda\nu}, \quad R = g^{\mu\nu} R_{\mu\nu}$$

The Kretschmann quadratic scalar invariant is:
$$K = R_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta} R^{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}$$

## 5. Reversibility Metrics

We strictly distinguish between two levels of reversibility:

1. **Manifold Coordinate Reversibility:**
   $$\epsilon_M = \|x - T^{-1}(T(x))\|_{\mathbb{R}^4}$$
   Measures whether a purely geometric coordinate transformation $T$ can be inverted analytically or numerically.

2. **Pipeline Reversibility:**
   $$\epsilon_P = \|x_0 - \Pi\left( \text{DAXDA}^{-1}(\text{DAXDA}(F(x_0))) \right)\|_{\mathbb{R}^4}$$
   Measures whether passing through the full 16-layer Clifford rotor transport and back preserves the original state coordinates.
